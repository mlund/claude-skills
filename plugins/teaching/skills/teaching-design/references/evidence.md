# Evidence

Effect sizes behind this skill, with *k* and caveats so claims can be weighed rather than quoted.
Where a number is contested or thin, it says so. Verify before citing in print.

## Layout and presentation

| Finding | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal contiguity (one step at a time, synced to speech) | **g = 0.78** [0.64, 0.92], k = 13 | Ginns 2006, via Noetel et al. 2022 |
| Contiguity overall | g = 0.74 [0.67, 0.82], k = 46 | Ginns 2006 |
| Spatial contiguity (labels on the graphic) | **g = 0.63** [0.55, 0.71], k = 58 | Schroeder & Cenkci 2018 |
| Signalling / cueing | **g = 0.43** [0.35, 0.50], **k = 209** | S. Schneider et al. 2018 (103 studies, N = 12,201) |
| — cognitive load | g = 0.25 [0.04, 0.45] | same |
| — **learning-relevant fixations** | g = 0.39 [0.09, 0.68] | same — the mechanism, measured |
| — dynamic vs static signals | d = 0.43 vs 0.38 overall | Alpizar et al. 2020 |
| — **expertise reversal** | low prior knowledge r = 0.19; **high r = −0.08** | Richter et al. 2016 |
| Modality (speech over on-screen text) | g = 0.38, k = 86; **0.20** after publication-bias correction | Reinwein 2012 |
| — holds **only system-paced** | d = 0.93; eliminated when self-paced | Ginns 2005; Reinwein 2012 |
| Segmenting | retention g = 0.32, transfer g = 0.36, load g = 0.23 | Rey et al. 2019 |

**Umbrella source:** Noetel et al. (2022), *Review of Educational Research* — 29 reviews, 1,189
studies, 78,177 participants. Its headline moderator: good design mattered **more for complex
material and in system-paced environments (lectures) than self-paced ones (websites)**.

Eye-tracking (Alemdag & Cagiltay 2018, via Noetel): when text and images were displayed, learners
**attended to text at the expense of images**. Text wins the attention contest by default.

## Animation and explorables

| Finding | Effect |
|---|---|
| Animation vs static, overall | d = 0.37 [0.25, 0.49], 26 studies, 76 comparisons (Höffler & Leutner 2007) |
| — **representational** | **d = 0.40** [0.26, 0.53], k = 59 |
| — **decorational** | **d = −0.05** [−0.37, 0.27], k = 17 |
| — the contrast between them | **z = 3.86, p < 0.001** — representational is *significantly better*, not merely "decoration does nothing" |
| — procedural-motor knowledge | d = 1.06, but **k = 5** — quote with its *k* |
| Animation vs static (second meta-analysis) | g = 0.226 [0.12, 0.33], 61 studies, N = 7,036 (Berney & Bétrancourt 2016) |
| Animation × spatial ability | high **d = 0.34**; low **d = −0.16** |

⚠️ In Höffler & Leutner, "video-based" and "photo-realistic" are **the same 12 comparisons** —
identical k, d and CI. Medium and realism are perfectly confounded; d = 0.76 cannot be attributed to
either. Median study n = 40.

## Activity, testing and guidance

| Finding | Effect |
|---|---|
| Active learning vs lecturing | **+0.47 SD** (k = 158); failure **OR = 1.95** (k = 67); 225 studies (Freeman et al. 2014) |
| Interpolated testing | mind-wandering 19% vs 39–41%; notes 24% vs 7–9%; **final-segment retention 84% vs 59%** (Szpunar et al. 2013, n = 48) |
| Interactive Lecture Demonstrations | up to 80% conceptual gain; **free-form 80% vs 5-choice clicker 59%** |
| Guided over pure discovery | consistent across problem-solving, conservation, LOGO (Mayer 2004) |
| Simulations need support | unsupported learners fail at hypothesis generation, experiment design, data interpretation, self-regulation (de Jong & van Joolingen 1998; Rutten et al. 2012) |
| Mastery / PSI gating | ~0.4–0.7 SD; reduced variance; largest gains for lowest prior attainment |
| Generative drawing | 26/28 experiments positive, median d = 0.40 (Fiorella & Mayer) |

⚠️ Szpunar's stimulus was a **slide** lecture (the note-taking measure is "% of slides with
additional notes"), so it transfers to this medium directly. Small sample.

## Games, gamification, curiosity

| Finding | Effect |
|---|---|
| Digital games vs non-game | ḡ = 0.33 [0.19, 0.48], k = 57 (Clark et al. 2016) |
| — augmented vs standard game design | ḡ = 0.34 [0.17, 0.51], k = 20 — **design matters as much as medium** |
| Serious games | learning d = 0.29, retention d = 0.36, **motivation d = 0.26 n.s.** (Wouters et al. 2013) |
| Simulation games | self-efficacy d = 0.52, declarative 0.28, procedural 0.37, retention 0.22 — with **"strong evidence of publication bias"** (Sitzmann 2011) |
| Gamification | cognitive g = .49, motivational g = .36, behavioural g = .25 — **only cognitive stable** under rigour filtering (Sailer & Homner 2019) |
| Curiosity and memory | elevated memory-area activity on **incorrect** guesses; durable recall for surprising answers (Kang et al. 2009); gains for target **and incidental** material (Gruber et al. 2014) |
| Verbal redundancy | **g = 0.29** adding key words to audio; **g = −0.04** adding audio to text (Adesope & Nesbit 2012) |

⚠️ Gamification meta-analyses reporting g > 1 (and one giving d = 4.18 for competitive games) should
be read as a warning about that literature, not as findings.

## Claims that do **not** hold

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Hard-to-read fonts aid learning (disfluency) | **Failed to replicate** — 13 experiments, >1,000 participants; "does not replicate, at least not in any general way" |
| Learner control helps | **g ≈ 0.05**; a second meta-analysis 0.02 across 62 studies |
| Interactive documents beat static | **Never tested head-to-head.** ICAP (Chi & Wylie 2014) is a framework validated on note-taking, concept mapping and self-explaining — not this comparison |
| Polling tools improve learning | Engagement and perception only; the one exam-score study found no effect |
| Puzzle-based learning has a higher-ed evidence base | Descriptive, small-scale, largely proponent-authored. Cite it for taxonomy; cite retrieval practice for effect |
