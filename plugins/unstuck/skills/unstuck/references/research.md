# Research and rationale

- **Productive struggle.** Warshauer (2015), *Productive struggle in middle school mathematics classrooms*, analyzed 186 episodes of struggle in proportional-reasoning tasks. Figure 1 and Table 2 describe a continuum of teacher responses: telling, directed guidance, probing guidance, and affordance. Teachers most often used probing guidance when students were unsure how to explain their thinking. When students hit a procedural impasse, teachers generally addressed that specific obstacle to help them resume the larger task. The skill adapts this distinction to chemistry. The paper is an exploratory classroom study. [DOI 10.1007/s10857-014-9286-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10857-014-9286-3) · [Strategies to Support Productive Struggle, companion article (PDF)](https://www.katm.org/s/Mid_ProductiveStruggle.pdf).
- **Productive failure.** This is a specific instructional design that starts with generating and exploring solutions and follows with instruction that builds on students' attempts. An early study used Newtonian kinematics. The skill borrows the idea of using an attempt to inform subsequent explanation. It can also teach a prerequisite immediately when a learner needs it. [Kapur (2008), Productive Failure (PDF)](https://arch.kuleuven.be/studeren/tall/artikels/productive-failure-kapur.pdf/%40%40download/file/Productive%20Failure%20Kapur.pdf) · [Sinha and Kapur (2021), meta-analysis (PDF)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3102/00346543211019105).
- **Scaffolding and metacognition in chemistry.** The Goldilocks Help workflow prompts novices to plan, monitor, and evaluate their problem solving. The skill adapts those prompts to the learner's immediate obstacle. [Yuriev et al. (2017), Goldilocks Help (PDF)](https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/10855/1/Goldilocks_paper_%28Final_Published_Paper%29.pdf) · [DOI 10.1039/C7RP00009J](https://doi.org/10.1039/C7RP00009J).
- **Instrumental help-seeking.** Roll et al. use this term for help sought in order to learn. Their geometry tutoring studies also describe *bottom-out hints*, which give away a step. The course's hint sequence stops before a hint does the student's work. [Roll et al. (2011), help-seeking and metacognitive feedback (PDF)](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/pubs/Roll%2C%20Aleven%2C%20McLaren%20%26%20Koedinger%202011.pdf).
- **Self-explanation.** Chi et al. prompted students to explain material about the circulatory system to themselves. In the skill, a short “why does that apply?” question helps surface how a student connects a principle to a decision. [Chi et al. (1994), Eliciting Self-Explanations Improves Understanding (PDF)](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chideleeuwchiulavancher_2.pdf).
- **AI tutoring and independent performance.** In a high-school mathematics field experiment, generic AI access improved practice performance while reducing subsequent unaided performance. The tutor with guardrails largely mitigated that decline. This motivates assessing whether students can move forward independently. The study tested a specific implementation in mathematics. [Bastani et al. (2025), full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/) · [DOI 10.1073/pnas.2422633122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122).
